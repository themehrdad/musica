# First post-release measurement — September 13, 2026

There is no evidence yet of improved acquisition. Exposure remains extremely low, and the sample cannot establish whether conversion improved or deteriorated.

App Store Connect history shows version 1.1 became Ready for Distribution on September 7 at 2:27 PM Pacific. Analytics currently run through September 12 and use UTC days. September 8–12 therefore provides five complete post-release reporting days. The comparison below uses the same weekdays one week earlier, September 1–5. September 6 and the partial release day September 7 are excluded.

| Recorded metric | Before: Sep 1–5 | After: Sep 8–12 |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Total impressions | 5 | 1 |
| App Store Search impressions | 5 | 1 |
| Product page views | 1 | None recorded |
| First-time downloads | None recorded | None recorded |

Source tables confirm all five pre-release impressions and the one post-release impression came from App Store Search. Daily tables show pre-release impressions on September 1 (3) and September 4 (2), and the post-release impression on September 9 (1). Search attribution does not reveal the exact query, organic rank, or whether exposure was paid. No ads account was audited.

The 90-day view covering the app's entire public lifetime shows 33 impressions, 12 page views, 3 first-time downloads, 1 redownload, 1 update, and 1 in-app purchase. All three first-time downloads occurred on August 27. Compared with the September 6 audit (reporting through September 4), impressions rose from 29 to 33: September 6 added two, September 7 added one, and September 9 added one. Only the final one belongs to the five full post-release days.

Apple renders days and source rows without reported activity as a dash. Those raw values are preserved in the accompanying JSON. “None recorded” describes the absence of additional reported activity; it does not turn unavailable or privacy-limited data into a verified numerical zero. Proceeds, paying users, subscription metrics, retention, and crashes remain Not Enough Data on the inspected overview. The one in-app purchase does not establish a paying subscriber.

The overview displays a 10.5% daily-average conversion rate across the 90-day window, versus 13.3% in the earlier audit. These are not matched post-release conversion rates and should not be used to claim the new listing hurt conversion. With one post-release impression, a meaningful conversion comparison is unavailable. There is also no new-customer cohort large enough to evaluate the 20-note allowance or rating-prompt changes.

## Next decisions

Keep the current listing and free allowance stable while acquiring relevant traffic. Another screenshot change or A/B test would not answer the present question with this volume. The immediate practical step is a small pilot with piano teachers and families: demonstrate the real-piano interaction, share the direct product link with their consent, and observe five first sessions. Record whether people understand the app, detect their first note, and reach their daily goal. Sending outreach still requires the user's explicit authorization for the recipients and message.

Repeat the metrics comparison once September 8–14 is fully reported; use September 1–7 as a clearly labeled transition-contaminated reference only if necessary, or retain the clean weekday-matched comparison above until a longer clean baseline is chosen. The first launch week included the initial download burst, so comparisons with it must not be presented as causal evidence. Separate partial days, redownloads, updates, and any paid exposure from first-time acquisition. A fresh-customer native iPhone search check remains necessary to establish actual query visibility; the aggregate impression is not a rank test.

No store metadata, prices, availability, app code, campaigns, or monitoring schedules were changed during this measurement. These files are local observations; no new commit or push was made.
